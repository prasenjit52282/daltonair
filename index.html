The Science Behind PoWear.

PoWear did not start as a product idea. It started as four years of published, peer reviewed research at the UbiNet Lab, IIT Kharagpur, on a simple question: people cannot see or feel indoor pollution building up around them, so how do we make it visible, understandable, and actionable? Here is the research trail, in order, that led to the wristband you can wear today.

All four studies share the same 89.1 million reading field deployment across 30 real indoor sites in India, so every finding below was validated at scale, not in a single lab room.

Chapter One: Mapping The Invisible Danger

DALTON

NeurIPS 2024, Datasets and Benchmarks Track

Before you can catch a dangerous spike, you need to know what one actually looks like in a real home. No dataset like that existed for developing communities, so the team built DALTON: Distributed Air quaLiTy mONitors, a six month field deployment of multi device sensor grids across 30 indoor sites: homes, studio apartments, canteens, classrooms, and research labs, in four regions of India.

Key findings

Multi device sensor grids

Every site logged eight pollutants plus temperature and humidity, with up to six devices per home, so researchers could watch a single pollution event travel from one room to the next instead of guessing from one sensor in a corner.

Real time activity labels

Activities were labeled in real time through a speech to text annotation app, so every pollution spike could be tied back to what people were actually doing: cooking, cleaning, sleeping, gathering.

Largest dataset of its kind

This is the largest indoor air pollution and activity dataset released for a developing nation, and it became the ground truth every later PoWear study was checked against.

02
Chapter Two: Why Pollution Hides Where It Does

Understanding Indoor Pollution Dynamics

ACM JCSS 2024

With DALTON in hand, the team asked a follow up question: where does pollution actually collect in a home, and why can't people just feel it? Analyzing airflow, floor plans, and occupant behavior across the 30 sites turned up patterns that most air monitors, and most people, completely miss.

Key findings

Bedrooms trap more than kitchens

In both summer and winter, isolated bedrooms accumulate more pollutant than the kitchen where the cooking actually happens, because closed doors and low traffic give it nowhere to go.

The overnight sink effect

Dirty dishes and food scraps left in the kitchen sink raise VOC and ethanol levels for hours, and the effect lingers until the next day even when nobody is home.

Isolated floor plans trap pollutants

Airflow directs where pollution spreads, so a sealed off kitchen or bedroom can hold onto VOCs long after CO2 has already cleared with basic window ventilation.

People ventilate for comfort, not safety

Occupants notice temperature changes and turn on a fan, but they cannot sense pollutants building up around them, so they stay unknowingly exposed until it is already a problem.

The opportunity this opened up: if people cannot feel pollution building up, could a wearable make it visible instead, and could directed airflow be used to clear it once it is found? That question became the next chapter.

Chapter Three: The First Wearable Prototype

CoWear, PoWear v1

ACM CHI 2026
CoWear is PoWear version v1.0 It carried a single CO2 sensor on the wrist, far fewer sensors than PoWear has today, built specifically to test one idea before adding anything else: if you can see where pollution is hiding, will you actually do something about it?

CoWear paired that wrist sensor with a smartphone AR app that renders CO2 as color coded, interactive bubbles floating in the room around you: blue for baseline air, green for moderate buildup, and pulsing red for a hazardous hotspot. An AR ventilation game then nudged users to point a fan at the red bubble instead of guessing where to open a window.

Key findings

Faster than a generic heatmap

Guided sessions cleared roughly 40 seconds faster per 100 ppm of CO2 than unguided ones, and the AR bubble consistently beat a generic heatmap.

Users felt successful

In post study interviews, most participants said they felt successful using it, wanted it in their own home, and would recommend it to a friend.

The concept was validated

That was the validation the team needed: the core wearable concept worked, and it was time to build the full sensor version people use today.

Chapter Four: Intelligence Without A Camera

PoHAR

IEEE DCOSS IoT 2026

CoWear proved people would act on what they could see. The next question was whether the wearable could also warn them proactively, before they even look, without turning into a surveillance device. PoHAR repurposes the same low cost air sensors as a privacy preserving substrate for human activity recognition: no camera, no microphone, just chemical diffusion gradients read straight off the air.

General activity accuracy
99.68%

Key findings

Beats cameras and mics on accuracy

99.68% precision beats 98.20% for video cameras and 91.40% for microphones, at zero privacy risk instead of extreme or high.

Three techniques, one ESP32

Self supervised air embeddings, RAFT leader election across the sensor network, and conflict free replicated sets keep every node in sync over a lossy connection, entirely on device.

Validated at DALTON's full scale

The whole pipeline was checked against 30 sites, 89.1 million samples, and 3,957 annotated events, so this is the intelligence layer that now runs quietly inside PoWear, without ever recording you.

How Four Papers Became One Wearable

This is what PoWear stands on.

DALTONshowed the team exactly what a dangerous spike looks like across 30 real homes, at a scale no prior dataset offered.
Pollution Dynamicsshowed where it hides, in bedrooms, sinks, and sealed off rooms, and proved people cannot feel it building up on their own.
CoWearvalidated the core wearable idea with a single sensor: shown an AR bubble instead of a beep, people cleared hazards 3.8x faster. That prototype became PoWear v1.
PoHARproved the same air chemistry can recognize what is happening in your home with over 97% accuracy, without a camera or a microphone.

Today's PoWear combines all four results into one wristband: the multi sensor grid pioneered in DALTON, the show don't just alarm interaction validated by CoWear, and the on device, privacy first intelligence from PoHAR, now shipping with more sensors than the original CoWear prototype.

DALTON // SPATIOTEMPORAL BASELINE

DALTON: Indoor Air Quality Grid.

Prior indoor pollution datasets relied on isolated single-room monitors. DALTON deploys multi-sensor spatial grids across 30 diverse Indian setups over 13,646 continuous hours. By pairing raw chemical sensors with speech-to-text Activities of Daily Living (ADL) annotations, we mapped how human habits actively contaminate living structures.

Critical Environmental Discoveries

The Deep-Frying Migration Hazard

Indian culinary habits frequently involve long-term deep frying with spices. This generates massive spikes in sub-micron particulate matter ($PM_{2.5}$) and Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs). Unlike boiling, which stays contained in the kitchen, frying aerosols aggressively migrate into bedrooms within 15 minutes.

The Overnight Ghost Effect

Unwashed dishes or food scraps left overnight in kitchen sinks cause a continuous, 8-hour rise in ambient VOC and Ethanol ($C_2H_5OH$) concentrations. Even with no active occupants, indoor air toxicity steadily degrades until the sink is sanitized.

The Architectural Trap

Floor plans with isolated kitchen structures trap VOCs aggressively. While $CO_2$ levels might quickly normalize via basic window ventilation, dangerous VOCs linger significantly longer, getting caught in architectural blind spots.

How Situated Awareness Drives Action (H1 & H2)

We hypothesized that making pollution visible at a personal, human scale would nudge occupants toward physical intervention. When CoWear detects local $CO_2$ exceeding 1200 PPM via its wrist-worn BLE transmitter, it pushes a spatial coordinate to the user's AR headset or smartphone.

  • Volumetric Color Coding: Blue spheres indicate baseline air ($<800\text{ PPM}$), green represents moderate accumulation ($800\text{--}1200\text{ PPM}$), and pulsing red spheres warn of hazardous stagnation ($>1400\text{ PPM}$).
  • Targeted Airflow Nudging: Guided by visual spheres, users do not just open windows blindly; they position desk fans to blow directly into the stagnant red bubble cluster, reducing clearance times from hours to seconds.

PoHAR // DECENTRALIZED EDGE INTELLIGENCE

PoHAR: Zero-Video Activity Tracking.

Traditional Human Activity Recognition (HAR) invades privacy using indoor cameras or microphones. PoHAR proves that ambient air quality networks can infer daily tasks without cameras. Every time an occupant sweeps, cooks, or sleeps, localized fluid diffusion signatures trigger our decentralized edge-AI pipeline.

The Decentralized Edge Intelligence Pipeline

Because standard IoT microcontrollers have tiny memory footprints (4MB Flash), they cannot process raw high-frequency sensor streams centrally. PoHAR introduces three novel algorithmic primitives running natively on ESP32 hardware:

01. Time-Frequency Consistency (TF-C) Embeddings

On-device self-supervised learning models compress multivariate time-series air data ($PM_{2.5}, CO_2, \text{VOC}, \text{Temp}, \text{RH}$) into compact, low-dimensional spatial vectors without cloud offloading.

02. Ultra-Lightweight RAFT Consensus

Distributed sensor nodes continuously elect a local "Cluster Leader" using heartbeat sync timeouts. Only the leader executes the final activity classification, reducing household energy draw by 68%.

03. Conflict-Free Replicated Data Types (Set-CvRDT)

Ensures seamless data merging across nodes even if household Wi-Fi drops or packets are delayed, maintaining 97.41% general daily activity accuracy.
